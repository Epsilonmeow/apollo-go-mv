# Sources, audio and visual interpretation

[中文](SOURCES.md) | English · [Project home](../README.en.md)

These references informed the original film. They support relationships between roles, equipment and operations; shots, layouts, terrain and most movement paths were authored for the film.

## Two clocks, two kinds of recording

**Film time** follows the musical edit of Go!; **GET** is Ground Elapsed Time, measured from mission launch. The song rearranges and repeats archival speech. Film seconds cannot be converted directly into GET, and repeated samples do not establish additional historical polls.

The soundtrack is **Public Service Broadcasting — Go!**. No separate NASA voice track was added. The commercial recording and historical recordings are different materials: the former supplied the musical edit; the latter helped identify speech and operational context. Neither is distributed in this repository.

An audio reference was the [Apollo Journals Flight Director loop](https://apollojournals.org/alsj/a11/A11_landing_FD_loop.mp3). [Honeysuckle Creek’s restoration account](https://www.honeysucklecreek.net/msfn_missions/MSFN/Network_A11_landing.html) documents NASA/JSC tapes 963-AAE and 964-AAE and Colin Mackellar’s speed, pitch and noise corrections. This is a restored archival copy, not audio extracted from the song.

Decimal cue times support repeatable animation; they do not imply millisecond certainty for every word onset. Audio matching, historical transcripts and later commentary are distinct forms of evidence.

## Distinctions retained in the film

1. **Go is an operational judgment.** It is not a remote switch that powers hardware. Permission to begin powered descent and later clearance to continue landing are different decisions.
2. **The LM and CSM have distinct systems and roles.** CONTROL and TELMU/TELCOM concern LM work; GNC and EECOM’s CSM responsibilities are not a duplicate LM panel. The film retains TELMU/TELCOM usage across its segments; neither should be read as a communications-only position.
3. **Alarms do not mean erased program code.** 1202 concerns unavailable core sets; 1201 concerns unavailable VAC areas. Protected recovery sustains essential work. Lower scheduling priority does not automatically mean unimportant work.
4. **People and computers continue working together.** P66 is not computer-free manual flight. Attitude first changes acceleration, then velocity; terrain judgment, instrument calls and throttle control serve different roles.
5. **Contact light, footpad loading and engine shutdown are distinct.** A probe touching the surface does not mean all four pads have settled. Lunar dust follows plume-driven ballistic motion, not an atmospheric smoke cloud.
6. **Landing is followed by a Stay decision.** Staying preserves ascent, rendezvous and return capability. It does not mean shutting everything down or beginning an EVA; T1 is not simply “touchdown plus one minute.”
7. **Three locations remain one mission.** Armstrong and Aldrin are on the surface, Collins is in orbit, and the ground team continues supporting them. Active communication paths respect lunar occultation.

## Scope of interpretation

Model detail, terrain, camera motion, diagram lengths, numerical-looking graphics, orbital phase and animation rhythm serve the film. They are not a complete engineering model, site survey or recovered telemetry. Earth–Moon space is compressed, and unnumbered diagrams explain relationships rather than compute actual flight states.

Each brief console graphic is an authored interpretation informed by the role and task. It does not claim that the controller explicitly stated every displayed reason during that Go. The ending does not introduce a new launch, rendezvous or EVA; retained routes represent future capability.

These distinctions remain in production documentation. The film itself proceeds through images, rhythm and sound without explanatory disclaimers interrupting the sequence.
